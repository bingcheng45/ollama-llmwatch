# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are acting in good faith. Criticise code, not the person who
wrote it.

This is a small project, and most of what happens here is someone saying "this number looks
wrong" and someone else finding out why. That works when both sides can be direct without it
being taken personally, and it stops working the moment anybody has to be careful about who they
are talking to.

## Expected

- Be direct about technical problems. "This is wrong, and here is the log line that shows it" is
  a good comment. Being right is not a licence to be unkind about it, and being kind is not a
  reason to be vague.
- Assume the other person knows something you do not. They usually do: a different terminal, a
  different Ollama version, a different model runner.
- Accept that maintainer time is finite. A pull request may sit, or be declined because it takes
  the project somewhere it is not going. Neither is an insult.
- Say when you are unsure. "I think this is a bug but I might be holding it wrong" is a perfectly
  good report, and it is how several real bugs arrived.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Sustained hostility about somebody's code counts.
- Demeaning comments about who someone is: age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or
  expression, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity.
- Unwelcome sexual attention or imagery.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or repeatedly reopening a settled decision to wear people
  down.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions and commit messages here, and anywhere someone
is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately through
[GitHub's private reporting](https://github.com/bingcheng45/ollama-llmwatch/security/advisories/new),
or by contacting the maintainer through their [GitHub profile](https://github.com/bingcheng45).
Reports stay private. If a report concerns the maintainer, GitHub's own
[report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) route goes over their head, which is the
point of mentioning it.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides what happens, proportionate to what occurred:

1. **A private word.** Most things stop here, usually because they were carelessness rather than
   intent.
2. **A public correction** in the thread, and the thread locked if it keeps going.
3. **A temporary block** from interacting with the repository.
4. **A permanent ban**, for sustained or severe behaviour.

Someone who reports something in good faith will not be worse off for it, whatever the outcome.
